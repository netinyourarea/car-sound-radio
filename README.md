# Drive Sound Studio

Create a completely original, premium website for **Satellite Car Audio Radio**. This website MUST NOT resemble a typical local-service campaign website or any of the previous cable/internet campaign websites. Treat this as a completely new brand and create a new visual identity from scratch. Do not reuse the same color palette, hero composition, section patterns, typography style, image style, card layouts, gradients, backgrounds, animations, or visual concepts commonly used in previous campaign websites.

IMPORTANT DESIGN RULE: Do NOT automatically choose the usual dark navy, blue, black-and-white, neon blue, purple gradient, or generic technology color scheme. Instead, create a fresh automotive-inspired palette using **warm ivory, soft cream, muted graphite, brushed-metal tones, deep olive/forest accents, and subtle amber/golden highlights**. The colors should feel sophisticated and connected to premium automotive interiors rather than telecommunications or software. Use the accent color sparingly. The website should have a warm editorial/automotive magazine atmosphere instead of a standard SaaS or campaign landing-page appearance.

Also, DO NOT reuse generic stock images commonly generated for car websites. Do not repeatedly show the same black sports car, generic dashboard close-up, person holding a phone inside a car, or generic car-at-night image. Every major visual should represent a different realistic automotive situation. Use varied imagery such as a detailed vintage-inspired car radio, a modern luxury vehicle interior, close-up physical audio controls, premium speaker components, a driver listening to music during a daytime road trip, a dashboard viewed from an unusual angle, classic radio equipment, modern infotainment controls, and detailed speaker installation imagery. Images should feel like carefully selected editorial automotive photography rather than generic AI stock photography. Never reuse the same image twice.

Keep the website relatively compact with only **Home, Services, About, and Contact** as the main pages, plus required legal pages. Do not create a huge website with unnecessary pages.

The **Home page must have a radically different hero design**. Do not use the standard “text on left + image on right” layout. Do not use a full-screen generic car background with centered text. Instead, create an **editorial automotive hero composition** inspired by premium automotive magazines and product campaigns. Use an asymmetrical layout with a large photographic vehicle/audio visual occupying an unusual portion of the screen, oversized typography positioned independently from the image, a small vertical label such as “CAR AUDIO / SATELLITE RADIO”, and a strong call-to-action integrated into the composition. Consider an offset image frame, layered photographic crops, oversized typography, or an intentionally asymmetric grid. The hero should immediately look different from a normal campaign website. The first screen should communicate premium in-car entertainment without looking like a telecom advertisement.

Use the headline **“MAKE EVERY DRIVE SOUND DIFFERENT.”** with a concise supporting statement about improving the in-car audio and listening experience. Add **Call Now — (855) 943-8332** as the primary action and **Explore Services** as the secondary action. Keep the typography bold and editorial, with large display text and smaller supporting copy.

Immediately after the hero, create an unusual **“Inside the Sound”** section rather than a normal About/Benefits section. Use a large close-up image of a physical car audio component, radio interface, speaker, or dashboard control. Place small numbered annotations around the image explaining different parts of the in-car audio experience. This should feel like an automotive product editorial rather than a collection of cards.

Next, create a **“Choose Your Listening Experience”** section using a horizontal editorial composition rather than cards. Present the services as large visual panels with different imagery and short descriptions. Include Satellite Radio Setup & Assistance, Car Audio Upgrades, Speaker & Sound Improvements, Radio & Infotainment Assistance, Audio System Troubleshooting, and Personalized In-Car Audio Solutions. Each service should have a DIFFERENT relevant photograph and its own visual treatment. Do not use six identical cards.

Create a distinctive **“From Radio to Road”** storytelling section showing the relationship between the vehicle, audio system, and driver. Use two or three different photographic compositions arranged asymmetrically across the page. Explain how better audio, properly configured equipment, and convenient assistance can improve the everyday driving experience. Avoid generic bullet-point benefit sections.

Add an **“Audio, Up Close”** section with an oversized detailed image of a car speaker, radio controls, or infotainment interface. Use small editorial typography to explain concepts such as clearer vocals, balanced sound, stronger bass, convenient controls, and an improved listening experience. Do not invent technical specifications or claim unsupported performance figures.

Create a **“Built Around Your Vehicle”** section that focuses on personalized assistance. Instead of icon cards, use a large vehicle image with a floating information panel or offset text block. Explain that different vehicles and audio setups can require different solutions and that customers can contact the team to discuss their setup.

Include a compact **service process** but make it visually unconventional. Do not use the standard three numbered cards. Instead, create a horizontal timeline or vertical road-like progression: **Tell Us About Your Vehicle → Understand Your Audio Needs → Find the Right Solution → Get Back to Enjoying the Drive**. Keep it simple and visually integrated into the automotive design.

Create a strong final CTA that does NOT look like every other campaign CTA. Do not use a giant gradient rectangle with centered text. Instead, use an immersive editorial composition with a large photograph extending beyond the section boundaries, a small heading, oversized typography, and a simple call button. Use the message **“YOUR DRIVE. YOUR SOUND.”** and **(855) 943-8332**.

The **Services page** should continue the same new visual identity. Begin with an editorial hero using a completely different image from the Home page. Then present each service as a large alternating image-and-content composition, but vary the layouts so they do not all look identical. Use relevant imagery for every service. Explain exactly what each service involves in clear customer-friendly language. Do not invent brands, certifications, warranties, years of experience, locations, awards, customer numbers, or technical claims.

The **About page** should feel like an automotive editorial story rather than a generic company profile. Use photography of car interiors, audio controls, or automotive details. Explain the purpose of Satellite Car Audio Radio: helping drivers improve and manage their in-car audio and satellite-radio experience. Avoid generic “Our Mission / Our Vision / Our Values” cards.

The **Contact page** should be visually simple but distinctive. Use a split editorial composition with a large automotive photograph, contact information, phone number **(855) 943-8332**, and a clean form containing Name, Phone, Email, Vehicle/Car Model, Service Needed, and Message. Do not invent a physical address or business hours. Make the phone CTA highly visible.

For imagery, prioritize **variety and authenticity**. Every major section must have a different image subject and composition. Do not use repeated images or visually similar AI-generated car photographs. Avoid the typical black luxury car parked on a dark road, blue neon dashboard, generic sports car, or generic front-facing vehicle. Use daylight, warm interior lighting, vintage automotive details, modern luxury interiors, close-up hardware photography, road-trip photography, and editorial automotive compositions. Images should look like they belong to one premium automotive publication while still being visually different from one another.

The website must NOT contain: blue telecom-style gradients, purple AI gradients, generic glassmorphism, excessive rounded cards, repeated three-card layouts, giant centered hero text, generic stock-business imagery, floating blobs, excessive glowing effects, repeated icon grids, unnecessary statistics, fake testimonials, fake reviews, fake awards, fake company information, fake addresses, or invented product specifications.

Use **editorial typography, asymmetrical grids, oversized headlines, thin dividers, photographic crops, unconventional spacing, layered compositions, and controlled transitions** to create the visual identity. Not every section needs a background color change. Let whitespace, typography, imagery, and composition create the hierarchy.

Animations should be subtle and intentional: image reveals, slow photographic movement, horizontal editorial transitions, text reveals, and small hover interactions. Do not animate everything. The website must remain fast and professional.

Make the entire website responsive and polished on desktop, tablet, and mobile. On mobile, preserve the unique editorial composition rather than simply stacking everything into generic cards. Optimize images, lazy-load non-critical imagery, maintain excellent performance, use semantic HTML, accessible contrast, proper heading hierarchy, descriptive image alt text, responsive typography, SEO-friendly metadata, clean URLs, and strong Core Web Vitals.

MOST IMPORTANT: Before generating the design, deliberately create a visual direction that is **fundamentally different from a typical campaign website**. Do not reuse design patterns from previous projects. Do not reuse previous campaign color palettes. Do not reuse previous hero structures. Do not reuse previous imagery. The result should look like a completely new automotive brand website designed by a different creative team.
